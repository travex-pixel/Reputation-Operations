# Customer conversation scripts

Natural, friendly, commercially credible. Say less than this; these are prompts, not lines. Adjust to how the person talks.

## 1. Warm referral request (text or short call to someone in your network)

> Hey [name], quick one. I'm starting something on the side: looking after Google reviews and customer follow-up for Sydney tradies, the plumbers and air-con guys who are great on the tools but never get round to replying to reviews or chasing the unhappy customer. I'm doing free one-page reviews of a few businesses to learn what they actually need. Do you know a tradie who'd be open to a 15-minute chat? No pitch, I just want to understand how they handle it. Happy to return the favour.

If they offer a name: "Would you mind texting them first so I'm not a cold call? Something like: 'My mate [founder] is doing free reviews of tradies' Google listings, worth a quick chat.' Then I'll follow up."

## 2. Initial introduction (email, after a warm intro or when the owner couldn't be reached by phone)

Subject: [Business name]'s reviews — quick observation

> Hi [first name],
>
> [Referrer] suggested I get in touch, or: I came across [business] while looking at [trade] businesses in [area].
>
> I run Reputrail, a small Sydney service that looks after Google reviews and customer follow-up for trade businesses: a person reads every review, drafts the reply for your approval, and rings you the same day if something needs handling.
>
> I noticed [one true, specific, non-judgemental thing: "you've got 84 reviews and a 4.8, but the last one was in June" / "there are a couple of two-star reviews from last year without a reply" / "your competitors on the Shire are at 200+ reviews"].
>
> I'm putting together free one-page audits for a handful of [trade] businesses: your rating, review count and reply rate against three competitors near you, what customers keep saying, and the three things I'd fix first. No obligation, no login needed, it's all public. Would you like one? I can also just call for 15 minutes to understand how you handle reviews now; that's genuinely useful to me either way.
>
> [Founder name]
> Reputrail · Sydney · [mobile] · reputrail.com.au

Rules: one observation, one offer, no attachments, no deck, under 150 words.

## 3. Phone script (business line, 7:30–8:30am or 3:30–5pm)

**Gatekeeper:** "Morning, it's [founder] from Reputrail in Sydney. Is [owner name] about? It's about the business's Google reviews, I'll be quick." If asked what it's regarding: "I've done a bit of a look at the business's reviews against a few competitors nearby and wanted to pass it on. Nothing to buy today."

**Owner answers:**

> Hi [name], [founder] here, I run a small Sydney service that looks after reviews and customer follow-up for tradies. Have you got two minutes? I'm not going to pitch you.
>
> I was looking at [business] against a few other [trade] businesses around [area] and noticed [the specific thing]. I'm curious how you handle reviews now: who sees them, who replies, what happens when a bad one comes in?

Listen. Use the discovery questions in `research/04-customer-validation.md`. Take notes verbatim.

**Close:**

> That's really useful, thanks. Can I do one thing for you? I'll put together a one-page audit, your numbers next to three competitors near you and what I'd fix first. It's free, it uses only what's public, and it's yours whether or not we ever talk again. What email should I send it to? And is there a better time to ring you if I've got a question?

If they ask what it costs: "If you ever wanted us to run it for you, plans start at A$299 a month, month to month, and the first three Sydney businesses get the full service for a month at that price. But let's see what the audit says first."

**No:** "No worries at all. Who else should I be talking to?"

## 4. Discovery meeting structure (20 minutes, phone or coffee)

| Minutes | Purpose | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Set the frame | "I want to understand how reviews and complaints actually work in your business. I'll shut up and listen. At the end I'll show you one page about your listing." |
| 2–12 | Discovery | Questions 1–12 from file 04. Follow the energy: the last bad review (Q4) is usually where the real story is. |
| 12–15 | Money and alternatives | Q10, Q11, Q14. Let silence do the work after Q14. |
| 15–18 | Show the audit | Walk the one page: where you stand, what we found, first 30 days. Ask: "Does that match what you see from the inside?" |
| 18–20 | Next step | If warm: "Want me to run this for a month? A$299, no lock-in, we'd start with the unanswered reviews." If not: "Who else should I talk to?" Confirm follow-up date. |

Never run past 20 minutes unless the owner extends it.

## 5. Follow-up message (same day)

> Hi [name], thanks for the time today. Attached is the one-page audit on [business]; the three things I'd fix first are on the bottom third. Two things you said stuck with me: "[quote]" and "[quote]". If you'd like us to take this on for a month, reply "let's do it" and I'll send the agreement and the Google manager invite instructions; it's a two-minute job on your end. If not, no hard feelings, and thanks again for the insight.
> [Founder]

Five working days later, one more touch: "Did the audit land? Anything you disagree with?" Then quarterly check-in only.
