# Personalised cold contact, compliant with Australian requirements

Cold contact is the fallback when no warm introduction exists. It is one specific observation, one offer of the free audit, and an easy way out. Not legal advice; see `research/08-compliance-and-risk.md` §3 and confirm with a lawyer before scaling.

## The rules we follow

1. **Phone first.** A call to a business's advertised line, during business hours, about the business, is ordinary B2B contact. The Do Not Call Register generally does not cover business numbers used for business, but check a number before dialling if it looks like a personal mobile, and never call a number marked private.
2. **Email only to published business addresses.** The Spam Act 2003 treats a commercial email as a commercial electronic message. Consent may be *inferred* where a business has conspicuously published its address and the message is relevant to that business's role (a published `info@` address on a plumber's website, for an offer about the plumber's reviews). It is not inferred where the publication says "no unsolicited commercial messages". Every message must identify who we are and how to contact us, and must include a functional way to opt out that works for at least 30 days. We honour opt-outs within five working days and record them.
3. **No SMS cold outreach.** Mobile numbers published for customers to book jobs are not consent to marketing texts. Do not text prospects.
4. **No personal data beyond the business.** Contact the business, not a named individual's private channels. Use the owner's name only where it is published on the business's own site.
5. **One observation, true and public.** Reference only what we actually saw on the public listing (rating, count, recency, an unanswered review). Never fabricate a problem.
6. **Two touches, then stop.** The initial message and one follow-up (the audit itself, if we made one). After that, a quarterly check-in list only.
7. **Keep a log.** Date, channel, what was sent, response, opt-out. The workspace prospect record holds this.

## Template email (under 150 words)

Subject: One observation about [Business]'s Google reviews

> Hi [first name if published, else "there"],
>
> I run Reputrail, a small Sydney service that looks after Google reviews and customer follow-up for trade businesses: a person reads every review, drafts the reply for your approval, and rings you the same day when something needs handling.
>
> Looking at [Business] alongside other [trade] businesses in [area], I noticed [one true observation, e.g. "your newest review is from March and two of your three negative reviews have no reply"].
>
> I'm preparing free one-page audits for a handful of [trade] businesses: your numbers next to three competitors near you, what customers keep saying, and the three things I'd fix first. It uses only what's public; no logins. Would you like one?
>
> If not, reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.
>
> [Founder name] · Reputrail · [business number] · reputrail.com.au · [business postal suburb]

## Template phone opener

> "Morning, it's [founder] from Reputrail in Sydney. Is [owner] about? It's about the business's Google reviews, I'll be quick and I'm not selling anything today."

Then the script in `business/customer-interview-script.md` §3.

## What we never do in outreach

Fake familiarity ("we've worked with businesses like yours" when we have not), invented statistics, scarcity that is not true, attachments, mass-mailing tools without opt-out handling, messages to personal mobiles, or contacting a business that has said no.
