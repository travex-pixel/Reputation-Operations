**Prepared for:** Reputrail (Sydney) — founder-led managed reputation & customer-experience service for Australian trade businesses, A$299–799/month.
**Prepared by:** Market research director, Reputrail
**Date:** 2 November 2026 (all URLs accessed 2026-11-02)
**Builds on (does not repeat):** `01-australian-market-analysis.md` (ABS 8165.0 counts, turnover bands, channels, review evidence), `02-trade-segmentation.md` (prior nine-criterion matrix that ranked HVAC first, plumbing second, painting third), `03-competitor-analysis.md` §3.22–3.25 (trade software, hipages, Oneflare, Google Business Profile).

## 0. Method and evidence quality

- **Brief.** Score twelve verticals out of 100 on seven weighted criteria, independently of the founder's starting hypothesis (painting) and of file 02's conclusion (HVAC). Every sub-score carries a one-line justification labelled **VERIFIED** (source URL; "snippet" where only a search-result extract was readable, "page read" where the page was fetched in full), **ESTIMATE** (our arithmetic from verified inputs), or **HYPOTHESIS** (a belief we could not source and that the validation process in §6 must test).
- **New research this session:** 41 web searches plus ABS ANZSIC class pages fetched in full (3299, 3242, 7311, 3234). Hosts blocked by the session network policy: data.gov.au (ATO business-industry-code list), jobsandskills.gov.au (pool-technician occupation profile). No page on hipages, IBISWorld, SPASA or ProductReview could be read in full; those are snippet-only.
- **Verticals without an ABS class.** Garage doors & gates, pool maintenance and property maintenance/handyman have no ANZSIC 4-digit class. ABS 3299 "Other Construction Services n.e.c." lists only metal wall cladding, petrol bowser installation, sand/steam blasting, scaffolding and waterproofing as primary activities — not garage doors or handyman work (VERIFIED, page read — https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/australian-and-new-zealand-standard-industrial-classification-anzsic/2006-revision-2-0/detailed-classification/e/32/329/3299). Swimming-pool cleaning is a named primary activity of 7311 Building and Other Industrial Cleaning (VERIFIED, page read — …/n/73/731/7311). Wooden flooring installation is a primary activity of 3242 Carpentry Services, *not* 3243 (VERIFIED, page read — …/e/32/324/3242), so "flooring" straddles 3243 (tiling, carpeting, floor sanding) and part of 3242. Business counts for these four verticals are therefore ESTIMATES or "not found", and their scores carry wider error bars than the eight ABS-classed trades.
- **Scoring noise.** Sub-scores are integer judgements. A total within ±3 points of another should be read as a tie; we say so wherever it matters.

## 1. Criteria and weights (as briefed)

| # | Criterion | Weight | What a 10 looks like | What a 0 looks like |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A | Reputation-management opportunity | 20 | Reviews decide which business gets the job; enough review flow for Reputrail to act on monthly; owner-owned Google profile is the shortlist filter | Work won by tender/sub-contract; reviews irrelevant |
| B | Customer pain and reputation risk | 20 | Frequent, visible, emotionally charged complaints (no-shows, price shock, scams) that a response/recovery desk can measurably reduce | Rare, contractual or defect-driven disputes beyond a call-back |
| C | Ability and willingness to pay | 20 | Majority of businesses turn over ≥A$200k, already spend on marketing, A$299–499/month is <1% of revenue | Sole traders under A$150k; fee is 3%+ of revenue |
| D | Recurring customer interactions | 15 | The client's own customers come back on a schedule (service plans, contracts), giving steady review triggers and year-round reasons to stay subscribed | One-off job every 7–10 years |
| E | Ease of reaching the decision-maker | 10 | Owner quotes in person, answers own mobile, decides same week | Office-fronted, B2B or franchise head office controls marketing |
| F | Competitive intensity (higher = less saturated) | 10 | Nobody sells marketing/reviews to these owners | Most marketed-to trade in Australia; owners field weekly agency calls |
| G | Operational simplicity | 5 | Complaints are service/pricing issues a 5–8 hr/week founder can resolve with a call-back | Technical/legal disputes (defects, warranties, insurance) |

Weighted total = Σ(sub-score × weight) ÷ 10, giving a mark out of 100.

**Cross-cutting evidence applied to every vertical.** ServiceTitan's 2025 survey of 1,025 Australian tradies found 70% market through word of mouth and 76% through their website, but only 29% use Google Ads, 17% SEO and 13% SMS (VERIFIED, snippet, vendor research — https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/australian-tradies-market-report-2025). Across all twelve verticals this supports a moderate competition score (most owners are *not* yet saturated with digital services) and tempers ability-to-pay (owners who spend nothing on digital rarely start at A$299). NSW Fair Trading publishes complaints only for businesses with ≥10 complaints in a month and we found no 2025 breakdown by trade (VERIFIED, snippet — https://www.cas.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/pubreg/FT_Public_Register.htm), so every pain score below rests on job economics and HYPOTHESIS rather than complaint counts.

**Reading the table.** Plumbing and HVAC are a statistical tie (1.5 points apart). Roofing and garage doors form a clear second tier (64–65). Painting sits ninth, 15 points behind the leaders — the gap is driven by two criteria with 35% of the weight (pay and recurring), not by any single judgement call. Flooring is last under every weighting we tried.

## 3. Sub-score justifications by vertical

Format: criterion — score — justification — label (source).

### 3.1 Plumbing (ANZSIC 3231) — 71.0

- **A Rep opp — 9.** Highest natural review flow of any trade: search-driven emergencies (blocked drains, hot water) create many small transactions; Oneflare shows 1,558 reviews across 45 Sydney plumbing businesses (~35 on-platform reviews each) and Airtasker 2,059+ reviews for Sydney plumbing at 4.94 average — on-platform volume that signals a review-heavy category (VERIFIED, snippets — https://assets.oneflare.com.au/plumber/nsw/sydney ; https://airtasker.com/au/services/plumbing/sydney). Job-count-driven volume estimate 60–200 reviews/yr for a 3-person firm (ESTIMATE, file 01 §9).
- **B Pain — 8.** Price shock vs quote and no-shows are the archetypal plumbing complaints; St.George found 43% of tradies cite slow-paying customers and 44% sourcing customers as top pains, both symptoms of trust friction (VERIFIED, file 01 §6). Emergency pricing disputes are frequent and public (HYPOTHESIS to test — we found no Australian complaint count by trade; NSW Fair Trading data by category was blocked, file 01 §12).
- **C Pay — 8.** 57.0% of plumbing businesses turn over ≥A$200k; 6,037 employing businesses in NSW; exit rate 11.9% (VERIFIED, ABS 8165.0 DC02/DC03 via file 01 §3.1). Call-out A$150–500, hot-water A$1.5–4k (file 02, ASSUMPTION). A$499/month is 0.7% of a A$850k firm (ESTIMATE).
- **D Recurring — 6.** Households re-use a trusted plumber but on no schedule; some firms sell hot-water/gas service plans (HYPOTHESIS). Scored above one-off trades, below contracted services.
- **E Reach — 6.** 42.5% non-employing and 47.8% with 1–4 staff in NSW means the owner usually answers, but is on the tools during business hours (VERIFIED counts, file 01 §3.3; ESTIMATE on who answers).
- **F Competition — 3.** The most marketed-to trade: hipages' largest categories, Google Ads agencies, SEO retainers, ServiceM8 review automation; Laser Plumbing network has 150+ Australian members with group marketing (VERIFIED, snippet — https://plumbingconnection.com.au/?p=7292 ; file 03 §3.22–3.23). Owners already hold many reviews, so the sale shifts from "get reviews" to "respond, recover, protect" (HYPOTHESIS).
- **G Ops — 6.** High volume and after-hours 1-star reviews raise delivery load; complaints are service/pricing, rarely legal (HYPOTHESIS; file 02 §5.2).

### 3.2 HVAC / air conditioning (ANZSIC 3233) — 69.5

- **A Rep opp — 7.** Big-ticket, quote-compared installs: 2.5 kW split A$1,200–2,500, 7 kW A$2,200–4,500, ducted A$8,000–22,000 (VERIFIED, snippet, commercial guide — https://whatsthedamage.com.au/air-conditioning-cost/). >70% of consumers are less trusting on jobs >A$2,500 (VERIFIED, hipages Trust Index, file 01 §4.2). Marked down from file 02 because 14.9% of HVAC businesses turn over ≥A$2m and the class includes refrigeration/coolroom and commercial contractors for whom Google reviews are secondary (VERIFIED share, ABS; ASSUMPTION on commercial mix).
- **B Pain — 7.** Peak-season delays, installer no-shows, warranty/callback and noise/leak disputes (HYPOTHESIS, file 01 §3.6). No Australian complaint count found.
- **C Pay — 9.** Highest ability to pay of any class: 64.0% ≥A$200k, 14.9% ≥A$2m, lowest non-employing share (39.3% NSW), lowest exit rate (10.4%) (VERIFIED, ABS via file 01 §3.1, §3.6).
- **D Recurring — 6.** Annual service recommended and priced A$120–220 (split) / A$250–480 (ducted) (VERIFIED, snippet — https://www.thequoteyard.com.au/services/air-conditioning-servicing-wa), but household uptake of annual servicing is unknown and probably low for splits (HYPOTHESIS). Scored equal to plumbing, down from file 02's 8.
- **E Reach — 6.** Owner or spouse in 1–4-staff firms; admin/office in 5–19-staff firms (HYPOTHESIS, file 02 §5.1). Seasonal: hard to reach Dec–Feb (HYPOTHESIS).
- **F Competition — 5.** Fewer agencies specialise in HVAC than plumbing (ASSUMPTION), but "air conditioning installation" is a heavily advertised Google Ads category and ARC-licensed firms are targeted by manufacturer dealer programs (HYPOTHESIS). Marked down from file 02's 7.
- **G Ops — 7.** Fewer, larger jobs than plumbing; complaints resolvable by a service visit (HYPOTHESIS).

### 3.3 Roofing (ANZSIC 3223) — 65.0

- **A Rep opp — 8.** High-value, infrequent, trust-poor purchases: restoration "from around A$2,500 … up to A$20,000 and beyond", re-roof A$15,000–40,000, minor repair A$250–800 (VERIFIED, snippets — https://www.canstar.com.au/home-loans/roof-restoration-cost/ ; https://whatsthedamage.com.au/roofing-cost/). A clean, responsive Google profile is the antidote to the category's scam reputation (HYPOTHESIS).
- **B Pain — 8.** Canstar: "due to a number of reported scams in the roofing restoration industry across Australia, authorities have warned consumers to be wary of 'fake tradie' scams" (VERIFIED, snippet — Canstar URL above). UK data (Which?) put roofers among the least trusted trades (48%) (VERIFIED, snippet, UK not AU — https://rcimag.co.uk/news/builders-roofers-considered-least-trustworthy-tradespeople). Leaks after repair and insurance-claim disputes are common (HYPOTHESIS).
- **C Pay — 7.** 57.8% ≥A$200k; 1,309 employing NSW businesses; exit 15.9% (higher churn than plumbing/HVAC) (VERIFIED, ABS via file 01 §3.1).
- **D Recurring — 3.** One-off and weather-triggered; gutter cleaning/maintenance plans exist but are a minority (HYPOTHESIS).
- **E Reach — 6.** Owner-operators quote on site; 43.8% non-employing (VERIFIED, ABS). Storm-season unreachability (HYPOTHESIS).
- **F Competition — 6.** Less agency attention than plumbing/electrical (ASSUMPTION); Dulux Acratex accredited-applicator program supplies some marketing to roof painters (VERIFIED existence, snippet — https://www.dulux.com.au/find-a-painter).
- **G Ops — 5.** Leak disputes can become insurance/defect arguments beyond a call-back (HYPOTHESIS).

### 3.4 Garage doors & gates (no ANZSIC class) — 64.0

- **A Rep opp — 7.** Urgent, search-driven ("door won't open, car stuck") plus considered purchases: motor replacement A$400–1,000 installed, spring pair A$280–550, minor repair A$110–430, Sydney double sectional A$4,025–8,600 installed (typical A$5,750) (VERIFIED, snippets — https://whatsthedamage.com.au/garage-door-cost/ ; https://whatsthedamage.com.au/garage-door-cost-sydney/). Google historically flagged garage-door repair as "high risk for scam" and added extra verification (VERIFIED, snippet, US, 2017 — https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/features/GoogleGDS_Win2017.pdf), so genuine reviews are a differentiator (HYPOTHESIS for Australia).
- **B Pain — 7.** Fake-listing and lead-hijacking spam is documented in the US garage-door category (VERIFIED, snippet — https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/features/WSJExposesFakeListings_Fall2019.pdf); in Australia the equivalent warning exists for locksmiths (SA Consumer and Business Services) and Thryv Australia names garage-door repair among affected small businesses (VERIFIED, snippets — https://cbs.sa.gov.au/news/south-australians-warned-to-be-on-the-look-out-for-dodgy-locksmiths ; https://www.thryv.com.au/?p=23156). Australian complaint data: not found.
- **C Pay — 6.** No ABS turnover data. Dealers combine retail margin and installation; a B&D or Steel-Line dealer with two vans is plausibly A$0.5–2m revenue (HYPOTHESIS). Many dealers receive manufacturer marketing (see F), which lowers *willingness* to pay a third party.
- **D Recurring — 5.** Annual service is recommended by providers and one consumer reports the B&D 10-year warranty being conditional on a paid annual service (VERIFIED, snippets — https://www.thequoteyard.com.au/services/garage-door-maintenance-sa ; https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/b-d-doors-and-openers — single consumer claim, not verified with B&D).
- **E Reach — 6.** Family-run dealers (e.g. "family owned & operated … Melbourne's South East with over 20 years") are reachable, but accredited dealers defer marketing decisions to the brand (HYPOTHESIS).
- **F Competition — 7.** Nobody sells reputation services to this trade (ASSUMPTION), but head-office marketing is strong: Steel-Line runs "over 20 branches and 200 resellers" (VERIFIED, snippet — https://archipro.com.au/professional/steel-line-garage-doors/info); B&D has accredited dealers in every state (VERIFIED, snippet — https://www.bnd.com.au/dealers-near-me/sa/), an unofficial directory lists 19 NSW dealers (VERIFIED, snippet, low-reliability source — https://directory.bnd.com.au/dealers/).
- **G Ops — 7.** Complaints are mechanical/warranty and scheduling — simple (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Market-size caveat:** business count not found. ESTIMATE from dealer networks: low hundreds of garage-door businesses in Greater Sydney — large enough for a founder, too small for a second vertical; see §5.

### 3.5 Pest control (ANZSIC 7312) — 60.5

- **A Rep opp — 6.** Routine, moderate-value jobs: general treatment Sydney A$205–520, termite inspection A$250–450, chemical barrier A$1,850–5,050 (VERIFIED, snippets — https://whatsthedamage.com.au/pest-control-cost-sydney/ ; https://whatsthedamage.com.au/pest-control-cost-adelaide/). Reviews matter against national brands (HYPOTHESIS).
- **B Pain — 5.** Complaints are re-infestation and scheduling; lower emotional charge than plumbing emergencies (HYPOTHESIS).
- **C Pay — 5.** 43.2% ≥A$200k; only 388 NSW businesses ≥A$200k; count shrinking −2.3% (VERIFIED, ABS via file 01 §3.10).
- **D Recurring — 9.** Annual termite inspections and quarterly/annual treatments are the business model (HYPOTHESIS, widely marketed).
- **E Reach — 6.** Owner-operators reachable; small pool.
- **F Competition — 5.** Search dominated by Flick Anticimex (50–60 branches, 780–1,000+ technicians), Rentokil (29 branches, 680 technicians) and Jim's Termite & Pest Control (VERIFIED, snippets — https://flick.com.au/about-flick/our-story ; https://www.rentokil.com/au/about/local-branches ; https://jims.net/how-to-start-a-pest-control-business/). Independents are under-served by agencies but out-marketed by brands.
- **G Ops — 8.** Simple complaints; low volume per client.

### 3.6 Landscaping (ANZSIC 3291) — 59.5

- **F Competition — 7.** Lightly marketed-to relative to plumbing/electrical (ASSUMPTION).

### 3.7 Electrical (ANZSIC 3232) — 59.0

- **A Rep opp — 6.** Electricians are rated the *most* trusted trade by consumers and tradies (VERIFIED, snippet, file 01 §3.4), and a material share work B2B (builders, strata, commercial, solar) where reviews are secondary (ASSUMPTION). Call-out A$50–150 + A$80–150/h (VERIFIED, snippet — https://whatsthedamage.com.au/electrician-cost/).
- **B Pain — 5.** Fewer price-shock emergencies than plumbing; safety-critical but low-dispute (HYPOTHESIS).

- **D Recurring — 5.** Test-and-tag, smoke-alarm and switchboard work recur loosely (HYPOTHESIS).
- **E Reach — 6.** As plumbing.
- **F Competition — 4.** Heavily marketed-to; Laser/O'Brien network, Jim's Electrical (advertising contribution ~A$620/month, leads A$27.50 — VERIFIED, snippet, company claim — https://jims.net/jims-electrical-franchise-better-leads/).

- **E Reach — 7.** Owner-operators; franchise territories are owner-run.
- **F Competition — 5.** Franchise-dense: Poolwerx ANZ A$205m turnover, 149+ stores, 501+ service vehicles, 353 territories (VERIFIED, snippet — https://www.businessfranchiseaustralia.com.au/?p=43464); Jim's Pool Care 160+ mobile franchisees (VERIFIED, snippet, Mar 2026 — https://www.splash.online/articles/jims-pool-care-reaches-160-australian-mobile-pool-shops); Swimart ~70 Australian stores (VERIFIED, snippet — https://insideretail.co.nz/?p=49323). - **G Ops — 8.** Simple.
- **Market facts:** ~1.36m residential pools attributed to SPASA's 2024 industry report (VERIFIED, snippet, secondary source — https://www.ctacquisitions.com/sell-your-business/australia/pool-service/); an older SPASA regulatory submission said ~1.2m (VERIFIED, snippet — https://aer.gov.au/system/files/D17-135118%20SPASA%20-%20Response%20to%20draft%20Demand%20Management%20Incentive%20Scheme%20and%20Innovation%20Allowance.PDF); Roy Morgan: 3.1m Australians live in a home with a pool or spa (VERIFIED, snippet — https://www.roymorgan.com/findings/9311-australian-swimming-pool-ownership-march-2023); new pool builds fell 31% y/y in the December 2024 quarter (VERIFIED, snippet — https://www.spasa.com.au/news/cost-efficiencies-allowing-more-aussies-to-make-a-splash). ## 4. The three strongest verticals, and the painting question

### 4.1 Top three

1. **Plumbing (71.0)** — wins on reputation opportunity and pain (the two criteria that define *why* a tradie would pay a human to handle reviews), with strong ability to pay and the largest serviceable pool (5,875 NSW businesses ≥A$200k, ~4,000 in Greater Sydney — VERIFIED/ESTIMATE, file 02 §2). Its weakness is competition: plumbers are the most-sold-to trade in Australia and many already have hundreds of reviews. Reputrail's plumbing pitch must be "respond, recover, protect", not "get reviews".
2. **HVAC (69.5)** — wins on ability to pay (64% ≥A$200k, lowest churn) and operational simplicity; loses ground to plumbing on review volume and on the commercial/refrigeration share of the class. Statistically tied with plumbing. Residential-focused HVAC firms with 2–19 staff remain an excellent target; the many firms that do both plumbing and air-conditioning make the two verticals one playbook.
3. **Roofing (65.0)** — the trade where a trustworthy, responsive public profile has the highest marginal value because the category carries a documented scam reputation and jobs are A$2,500–40,000. Weaknesses: one-off work, weather-driven unreachability, and leak/insurance disputes that can exceed a part-time founder's remit. Roofing is a strong third, not a co-leader.

**Challenger: garage doors & gates (64.0).** Urgent, search-driven, spam-afflicted, with a service/warranty recurrence hook — but no business-count data and heavy manufacturer marketing. Worth ten discovery calls, not a cohort of its own yet.

### 4.2 Is painting objectively weaker than the leaders? Yes.

Painting scores 56.0 — ninth of twelve and 15 points behind plumbing. The gap is not a matter of taste:

- **Ability to pay (20% weight):** 37.9% of painting businesses turn over ≥A$200k versus 57.0% (plumbing), 64.0% (HVAC) and 57.8% (roofing); 69% are non-employing versus 39–44% (VERIFIED, ABS). A A$299 fee is 3% of a A$120k sole trader's revenue and the median painter is closer to that than to A$400k (ESTIMATE).
- **Recurring interactions (15% weight):** repaint cycles are 7–10 years (ASSUMPTION); there is no service contract, no annual visit, no emergency flow. Painting scores 2 where plumbing/HVAC score 6.
- **Reputation opportunity:** painting's reviews matter (quote-shopped, discretionary) but the *flow* is thin — 4–16 reviews a year for a small crew (ESTIMATE). A managed service that monitors, responds and escalates has little to do; the only high-value component is the request workflow, which ServiceM8 and Jobber already automate for ≤A$120/month (VERIFIED, file 03 §3.22).
- **Where painting genuinely wins** — reach (8), operational simplicity (8), low competition (7) — carries only 25% of the weight, and even the founder-constrained weighting (S4) that doubles reach and triples ops leaves painting seventh.

File 02 reached a similar conclusion ("well matched to the founder, poorly matched to the price") but ranked painting third; under the brief's weights that ranking does not survive. Painting should be a design-partner segment (2–3 painters for the request workflow) and a candidate for a lighter A$149–249 tier later — not a launch vertical.

## 6. Thirty-day customer-validation process for the top three

**Objective.** Decide between plumbing, HVAC and roofing (with garage doors as a side-bet) by 2 December 2026 using paid commitments, not opinions.

### 6.1 Cohorts

| Cohort | Definition | Source list | Target conversations (30 days) | Audits delivered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P — Plumbing | Greater Sydney residential plumbing businesses, 2–19 staff, Google profile with ≥20 reviews (so there is something to manage) | Google Maps by suburb cluster; Master Plumbers NSW directory; hipages/Airtasker Sydney listings | 10 discovery calls (≥6 with the owner) | 5 fifteen-minute profile audits |
| H — HVAC | Greater Sydney residential air-conditioning installers/servicers, 2–19 staff, ARC-licensed, residential work ≥50% of jobs (ask) | ARC licence search; manufacturer dealer locators (Daikin/Fujitsu/Mitsubishi — ask, not scrape); Google Maps | 10 discovery calls | 5 audits |
| R — Roofing | Greater Sydney roof repair/restoration firms, 2–19 staff, residential | Google Maps; Dulux Acratex applicator locator; Master Builders/HIA roofing members | 10 discovery calls | 5 audits |
| G — Garage doors (side-bet) | Independent and accredited-dealer garage-door firms, Greater Sydney | B&D/Steel-Line dealer locators; Google Maps | 5 discovery calls | 2 audits |

Total: 35 conversations and 17 audits in 30 days — roughly 9 conversations and 4 audits a week at 20–30 minutes each, i.e. 4–5 hours a week plus 1–2 hours of list-building. This fits a 5–8 hr/week founder only if delivery for existing clients is near zero, which it is at this stage.

**Channel test built in.** For each cohort, log how the owner was reached (cold call to mobile, trade-counter introduction, Facebook group, referral from accountant/supplier, LinkedIn) and the number of touches to a 20-minute conversation. This directly tests criterion E, where every top-three score is a HYPOTHESIS.

### 6.2 The three discriminating questions (asked identically in every call)

1. **Volume and handling (tests A):** "In the last 90 days, how many Google reviews did you get, how many were under 4 stars, and who — if anyone — replied to them?" Record counts; verify against the live profile after the call.
2. **Pain and process (tests B and G):** "When a customer is unhappy, how do you usually find out, how long does it take, and what happens next?" Score 0–3: 3 = finds out from a public review, no process; 0 = has a documented recovery process and someone who runs it.
3. **Willingness to pay (tests C):** "You're spending roughly A$___ a month on hipages/Google Ads/other. If a Sydney-based service made sure every customer was asked for a review, every review got a reply in your voice that you approve, and every unhappy customer got a phone call within 24 hours, what would that be worth to you a month — and would you take a 90-day paid pilot at A$299?" Record the stated number, the current marketing spend, and the yes/no/maybe.

### 6.3 Pass/fail thresholds per cohort

| Metric | Pass | Fail | Why this threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach: owner conversations achieved in ≤2 touches | ≥60% of attempted owners | <40% | A part-time founder cannot sustain a 5-touch cycle |
| Volume: median reviews in last 90 days (verified on profile) | Plumbing/HVAC ≥6; Roofing ≥3 | Plumbing/HVAC <3; Roofing <1 | Below this Reputrail has nothing to manage monthly |
| Unmanaged pain: share of owners scoring 2–3 on Q2 | ≥50% | <30% | The recovery desk is the differentiator; if owners already handle it, the pitch is only software |
| Stated willingness: share naming ≥A$299/month on Q3 | ≥50% | <30% | Half the room at list price indicates a real segment, not a discount hunt |
| Current spend: share already spending ≥A$300/month on marketing | ≥50% | <30% | Reputrail displaces or complements existing spend; owners spending nothing rarely start at A$299 |
| **Paid pilots signed by day 30 (≥A$299, 90 days, card on file)** | **≥2** | **0** | The only metric that cannot be faked by politeness |

### 6.4 Decision rule

- **The first vertical to produce two paid pilots at ≥A$299/month wins the launch slot** and becomes the sole focus for days 31–120.
- If two verticals both reach two pilots, pick the one with the higher median *stated* price on Q3 (less discounting ahead) and the lower touches-to-conversation.
- If no vertical reaches two pilots by day 30 but one has ≥1 pilot and passes four of the five soft thresholds, extend that cohort alone by 14 days.
- If no pilots at all: the problem is the offer, not the vertical. Re-test with the A$499 tier framed around the recovery desk (missed calls, unhappy-customer call-backs) rather than reviews, per file 03 §1 point 7, before touching painting.
- Garage doors (G) graduates to a full cohort in month two only if ≥2 of 5 owners name ≥A$299 on Q3 and the owner — not a manufacturer — controls the Google profile.
- Painting is not in the test. The only trigger for re-opening it: a painter approaches Reputrail unprompted and pays A$299 without negotiation (file 02 §6.2 standard).

### 6.5 What would change this ranking

- If Q1 shows Sydney residential HVAC firms receiving ≥10 reviews per quarter and plumbers mostly already using ServiceM8/Jobber automation with managed replies, HVAC overtakes plumbing on criterion A and F (its scores were held down by the commercial-mix and ad-saturation assumptions).
- If roofers prove unreachable (reach <40%) or name insurance disputes as their main pain, roofing drops behind garage doors and pest control.
- If plumbers say reviews are "handled" by their software but still score 2–3 on Q2, the plumbing pitch narrows to the recovery desk — still viable, but at A$499, not A$299.
