## 1. Executive summary

1. **The addressable universe is large, fragmented and owner-operated.** At 30 June 2025 Australia had 453,030 Construction-division businesses by June 2026 (VERIFIED, ABS) and the eleven trade/home-service ANZSIC classes we profile total roughly **300,000 businesses nationally and ~100,000 in NSW** (ESTIMATE, sum of ABS class counts below). Between 41% (air-conditioning) and 72% (cleaning) of them have **no employees**; the majority of the remainder employ 1–4 people.
2. **Ability to pay varies sharply by trade.** The share of businesses turning over ≥A$200k is 64% in air-conditioning & heating, 57–58% in plumbing and roofing, 54% in electrical, 51% in landscape construction, but only 38% in painting, 26% in cleaning and 31% in gardening (VERIFIED, ABS turnover cube). A A$299–799/month fee is 0.7–1.9% of revenue for a A$500k business and 3.6–9.6% for a A$100k sole trader — the latter will not buy (ESTIMATE).
3. **NSW is the largest state market for plumbing, electrical, HVAC, roofing, painting and pest control**, and Greater Sydney (incl. Central Coast) holds ~69% of NSW construction businesses (VERIFIED, derived from ABS SA2 cube). That puts roughly **7,200 plumbing, 11,200 electrical, 1,900 air-conditioning and 5,700 painting businesses in Greater Sydney** (ESTIMATE).
4. **Reviews matter and are regulated.** International survey data (BrightLocal, US sample) shows ~93–97% of consumers read local-business reviews and ~80% are more likely to use a business that responds to all its reviews (VERIFIED, secondary sources). Australian-specific evidence is thinner: a hipages Tradie Trust Index found 46% of consumers trust online testimonials and >70% are *less* trusting of tradies on jobs above A$2,500 (VERIFIED, snippet, undated). The ACCC's 2023 sweep found 37% of 137 businesses engaged in concerning review conduct, and flagged **home improvement** as a highest-risk sector — meaning Reputrail's "owner-approved, genuine-review-only" posture is both a compliance necessity and a selling point (VERIFIED, snippet).
5. **Tradies already pay for demand generation.** hipages' CEO put average customer spend at ~A$120/month with plans from A$49 to thousands per month (VERIFIED, snippet); hipages had ~33,000–35,000 subscription tradies in FY25 (VERIFIED, snippet). The category Reputrail competes with for wallet share is lead-generation, not "reputation management" as such.
6. **Pain points are stable over time:** sourcing new customers (44%), hiring (44%), slow-paying customers (43%) and cashflow (36%) in the St.George Tradie Economy Report (VERIFIED, 2019, 600+ tradies). Reputrail should be sold as a *customer-sourcing and customer-retention* tool, not as "marketing".
7. **Software adoption is partial and fragmented** (ServiceM8, Tradify, Fergus, simPRO/AroFlo, Xero). No credible Australian adoption survey was found; third-party estimates suggest Tradify ~20k customers globally with ~40% in Australia (VERIFIED, third-party estimates only). Most of these tools can send an automated review request; none provides a human-managed response/escalation service, which is Reputrail's wedge.
8. **Competition for a human-managed reputation service for Australian tradies is thin.** Global SaaS (Birdeye US$299–449/month, Podium US$399–599/month) is priced for multi-location firms and is self-serve; local SEO agencies sell reviews as a bolt-on to A$1,000+/month retainers; cheap tools (A$29–69/month) exist but are DIY. (VERIFIED, snippets; §10.)
9. **Industry is in a mild cyclical trough but structurally short of labour**: IBISWorld estimated 2025 revenue declines of ~4–6% in plumbing, electrical, painting, HVAC and roofing services (VERIFIED, snippets), while HIA puts the residential-construction skills shortage at ~83,000 workers (VERIFIED, snippet, Sept 2026). Short labour + soft demand = owners are motivated to win *better* jobs rather than *more* jobs, which favours a reputation proposition.
10. **Recommendation (detailed in file 02):** target employing (1–19 staff) residential **air-conditioning/HVAC** and **plumbing** businesses in Greater Sydney first; keep **painters** as a well-matched but price-constrained second-wave segment; defer renovation builders, cleaners, gardeners, tilers and carpenters.

## 2. Macro context

### 2.1 Business counts (ABS)

- Australia had **2,814,778 actively trading businesses at 30 June 2026**, of which 996,203 were employing; non-employing businesses grew 4.8% (+83,105) in 2025-26 and 689,600 businesses had 1–4 employees. **VERIFIED** — ABS, *Counts of Australian Businesses, including Entries and Exits, July 2022 – June 2026*, released 18 Aug 2026 (full page read): https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/latest-release
- **Construction division: 453,030 businesses at 30 June 2026** (up from 444,070; +2.0%), with an entry rate of 17.3% and exit rate of 15.2% in 2025-26. **VERIFIED** — ABS data cube 1, Table 1 (file read): https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/jul2022-jun2026/8165DC01.xlsx
- **NSW: 942,658 businesses at 30 June 2026** (+2.8% in the year; entry rate 16.9%, exit rate 13.9%). **VERIFIED** — same cube, Table 4.
- Class-level (4-digit ANZSIC) counts by state are only published in the December data cubes; the latest available at the time of writing is **30 June 2025** (released 16 Dec 2025). All class-level figures in §3 are therefore at June 2025. **VERIFIED** — https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/business-indicators/counts-australian-businesses-including-entries-and-exits/jul2021-jun2025/8165DC02.xlsx (employment size) and …/8165DC03.xlsx (turnover size), both downloaded and read in full.

### 2.2 Building activity (demand backdrop)

- June quarter 2026: value of new residential building work done A$23,644.9m (chain volume, seasonally adjusted; +8.0% y/y); **alterations and additions to residential buildings A$4,119.0m for the quarter (+12.6% y/y)**; 52,201 dwelling commencements nationally, 11,252 in NSW. **VERIFIED** — ABS *Building Activity, Australia*, June 2026 (full page read): https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/building-and-construction/building-activity-australia/latest-release
- **ESTIMATE:** annualising the quarterly A&A figure gives a residential renovation construction market of roughly **A$16–17bn/yr** in work done (4 × A$4.12bn), excluding owner-purchased materials and small jobs under approval thresholds. This is the pool that painters, renovators, tilers, electricians and plumbers draw on alongside repair/maintenance work.

### 2.3 Labour market

- Nearly half of all trade occupations remained in national shortage on the latest Jobs and Skills Australia Occupation Shortage List, with electricians and plumbers flagged as persistent shortages. **VERIFIED (snippet only)** — https://www.hcamag.com/au/news/general/these-jobs-may-see-big-pay-bumps-and-influencers-are-telling-people-how-to-get-them/583128 and https://totalvettrainingresources.com.au/blog/jobs-and-skills-australia-2025-occupation-shortage-lists-trades-and-technical-skills/

- Victorian Skills Plan medians: plumbers A$98,000, electricians A$97,000, painting trades workers A$74,000. **VERIFIED (snippet only)** — https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-04/fact-sheet-construction-victorian-skills-plan-for-2025-into-2026.pdf
- HIA report *Australia's Future is Built by Tradies* (Sept 2026): shortage of ~83,000 skilled workers across residential construction. **VERIFIED (snippet only)** — https://hia.com.au/our-industry/newsroom/skills-training-and-workforce/2026/09/new-report-outlines-path-to-tackling-australias-tradie-shortage
- Direct JSA headcounts for plumbers, electricians and air-conditioning mechanics: **Not found in available sources** (jobsandskills.gov.au was blocked in this session). IBISWorld industry employment figures are given per trade below as a proxy, but they count industry employment, not occupation headcount.

### 2.4 Industry cycle

IBISWorld summary pages estimated 2025 revenue declines across most building-services industries: plumbing −6.4%, electrical −5.0%, painting −5.7%, air-conditioning & heating −4.4%, roofing −4.4%, landscaping −2.8%; commercial cleaning +1.9% and pest control +2.3% grew. **VERIFIED (snippets only; commercial estimates)** — URLs in §3 per trade. Note that ABS *business counts* nonetheless grew in every class except pest control in 2024-25 (§3), i.e. more businesses chasing slightly less revenue — a competitive environment that raises the value of differentiation on reputation.

### 5.3 Regulatory environment (ACCC / Australian Consumer Law)

- ACCC sweep (Dec 2023) of 137 businesses: 37% engaged in concerning conduct around reviews/testimonials; highest-risk sectors included **home improvement and household products and services**; most businesses and platforms did not disclose incentivised reviews; undisclosed incentivised reviews are "likely to mislead". **VERIFIED (snippets)** — https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/scrutiny-of-influencers-and-businesses-for-misleading-advertising-and-online-reviews-continues ; https://www.mondaq.com/australia/consumer-trading-unfair-trading/1413158/accc-review-of-australian-social-media-influencer-testimonials-and-online-reviews-highlights-concerning-practices
- ACCC guidance: reviews must be genuine; selectively removing/suppressing negative reviews is treated as misleading, as is posting fake positive ones; incentives (if any) must be offered regardless of sentiment and disclosed; example enforcement: removalist Citymove paid a A$6,600 infringement notice. **VERIFIED (snippets)** — https://accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/small-business-education-program/misleading-conduct-and-advertising/online-reviews-must-be-genuine ; https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/online-review-and-testimonials.pdf ; https://lawpath.com.au/blog/how-businesses-can-make-sure-their-online-reviews-comply-with-the-acl
- **Implication (RECOMMENDATION):** Reputrail must (a) never gate review requests by sentiment ("ask everyone"), (b) never offer undisclosed incentives, (c) handle negative feedback by *resolution and response*, not removal (except genuinely policy-violating reviews via platform flagging), and (d) make the owner approve every response. This is a compliance moat against "buy Google reviews" operators and a reason for trade associations to endorse the service.

## 11. Sales cycle, recurring revenue, delivery complexity and operational risk

- **Sales cycle (HYPOTHESIS, to be measured):** owner-operators decide fast (same week) if reached in person or by referral; hipages-style subscription habits (6–12-month terms) show tradies accept monthly commitments for demand-related services (VERIFIED, §4.1). Expect 2–4 touches, with a free "reputation audit" (current rating, review count vs. 3 local competitors, response rate) as the opener (RECOMMENDATION).
- **Recurring revenue potential:** strongest where the *client's own* customers recur (HVAC servicing, pest, pool, cleaning) and where review volume is continuous (plumbing, electrical). Weakest where jobs are one-off and seasonal (painting, roofing, landscaping construction, renovation) — these clients may pause after a quiet quarter (HYPOTHESIS).
- **Delivery complexity:** proportional to (a) review volume, (b) share of negative reviews, (c) technical/legal content of disputes. Renovation/building and solar disputes often involve defects, contracts and warranties (ASSUMPTION) and can exceed what a 5–8 hr/week founder can safely handle; plumbing/HVAC/pest complaints are mostly service/pricing issues resolvable by a call-back and a goodwill gesture.
- **Operational risks:** (1) ACL compliance — mitigated by owner approval and no sentiment-gating (§5.3); (2) Google policy changes/API access; (3) client churn from business exit — mitigate by targeting employing businesses (§3.2); (4) seasonality of demand in HVAC and painting; (5) key-person dependence on founder time; (6) platform concentration (Google) and the Oneflare shutdown (§4.1).
