# 04 — Customer validation plan

Purpose: before selling, have ten genuine conversations with Sydney trade owners and learn how they actually handle reviews, complaints and follow-ups. The deliverable from this phase is evidence, not customers; customers are the by-product.

## Hypotheses we are testing

| # | Hypothesis | Type | How we'll know |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Owners check Google reviews reactively (when a notification arrives) and reply to fewer than half. | HYPOTHESIS, supported by BrightLocal reply-rate data (US) and the example profiles seen in prospecting | ≥7 of 10 owners say they reply to "some" or "none"; we see unanswered reviews on their profile |
| H2 | The owner personally handles complaints, usually by phone, and finds it the most stressful part of the week. | HYPOTHESIS | Unprompted mention of stress/anger; the owner, not staff, is named as complaint handler |
| H3 | Nobody systematically asks happy customers for reviews; the ones who do use their job software's automation and have stopped looking at it. | HYPOTHESIS; file 01 §8 | ≤3 of 10 have a consistent ask; those who automate can't say their last month's request count |
| H4 | Owners believe reviews affect quote conversion but can't quantify it. | HYPOTHESIS; hipages Trust Index (46% trust testimonials; >70% less trusting on jobs >A$2,500) | "Yes, definitely" plus an anecdote, no number |
| H5 | Owners already spend A$100–800/month on demand generation (hipages, Google Ads) and will compare Reputrail to that, not to "marketing". | VERIFIED spend benchmarks (file 01 §4.1, §7); HYPOTHESIS on framing | They name hipages/Ads spend when asked about marketing budget |
| H6 | The willingness to pay A$299–499 exists for employing HVAC/plumbing firms and not for sole-trader painters. | HYPOTHESIS from file 02 | Reaction to the pilot price by segment |
| H7 | Owners will not hand over passwords but will add a manager to their Google Business Profile once shown how. | HYPOTHESIS; Google roles VERIFIED (file 08 §4.3) | Objection appears; resolves when the manager invite is demonstrated |
| H8 | The phrase "someone who picks up the phone" lands harder than "AI-powered reputation management". | HYPOTHESIS | Which phrasing gets the "yeah, that" reaction |

## The 15 discovery questions

Ask in order, conversationally. Do not pitch until question 15 is answered. Write down exact words.

1. When was the last time you looked at your Google reviews, and what made you look?
2. Who in the business sees a new review first, and how do they find out?
3. Roughly how many of your reviews have a reply from you? What stops you replying to the rest?
4. Tell me about the last bad review you got. What happened, what did you do, and how did it end?
5. When a customer is unhappy, who handles it, and how do they usually find out the customer is unhappy?
6. Have you ever lost a customer or a repeat job because a follow-up slipped? How would you even know?
7. Do you ask customers for a review after a job? Who, when, how, and how consistently?
8. If I asked you to find a customer's contact details from a job six weeks ago, how long would that take you? (Probe: which software, if any.)
9. When you quote against two other businesses, how often do you think the customer checks your reviews first? Has a customer ever mentioned a review to you, good or bad?
10. What do you spend each month on getting work in: hipages, Google Ads, a website person, an agency, vehicle signage? Which of it do you think works?
11. Do you work with a marketing or SEO agency now? What do they do for you, and what do you wish they did?
12. If a review turns up at 9pm on a Friday saying your apprentice left a mess, what do you want to have happened by Monday morning?
13. What is the one admin job you would most like to never do again?
14. If someone you trusted looked after your reviews and rang you only when it mattered, what would you expect to pay for that each month? (Let them answer before mentioning any number.)
15. If I did a free one-page audit of your reviews against three competitors near you, would you want to see it? Who else should I talk to?

## Who to interview

Ten conversations in the first 30 days, in this mix: four air-conditioning/HVAC owners, three plumbers, one electrician, one painter, one roofer or renovator. Prefer 2–20 staff, Greater Sydney, owner still on the tools or managing jobs. Sources, in priority order: warm introductions from the founder's network, suppliers' trade counters (Reece, Tradelink, Beacon Lighting, Dulux Trade Centres), Sydney trade Facebook groups, and the prospect list.

## Interview logistics

- Fifteen to twenty minutes by phone, or a coffee on site. Never ask for more than 20 minutes.
- Record only with permission; otherwise type notes within an hour.
- After each call, update the hypothesis table above: supported, contradicted, or unclear, with a quote.
- Offer the free audit at the end of every call regardless of fit. The audit is the product demo.

## What a "validated" result looks like at day 30

- At least 6 of 10 owners describe reviews as affecting quotes (H4), and at least 5 reply to fewer than half (H1).
- At least 5 of 10 accept the free audit; at least 2 ask about price before we raise it.
- At least one owner says a version of "I'd pay for that if I knew it was handled" (H8).
- Pilot price reaction: no more than 2 of the HVAC/plumbing owners call A$299–499 "too much" for a managed service (H6).

## Kill or pivot signals

- Owners consistently say they have the time and do reply to everything: the pain is smaller than assumed; pivot to the adjacent "missed-call and follow-up" problem (file 03 Q6).
- Owners consistently refuse to add a manager to their Google Business Profile: trust is the blocker; pivot to coaching/templates rather than managed publishing.
- Price reaction is uniformly "A$100 a month at most": the segment can't pay; move up-market (multi-crew, multi-location) or down to a monitored tier.
