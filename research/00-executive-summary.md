# Reputrail — Executive summary

Prepared 2 November 2026. Labels used throughout the research pack: **VERIFIED** (sourced, URL given), **ESTIMATE** (derived from verified inputs, method stated), **ASSUMPTION** (unverified premise we are proceeding on), **HYPOTHESIS** (to be tested with customers), **RECOMMENDATION** (our judgement).

## Research limitations, stated up front

- Live web research ran through a restricted network. Australian Bureau of Statistics data cubes and Anthropic's legal and pricing pages were read in full. Most commercial, regulator and competitor sites (ACCC, OAIC, ACMA, Birdeye, Podium, hipages, IBISWorld, Google support) could only be read through search-result snippets. Every such claim is labelled "snippet-only".
- The web-search budget for the session was exhausted mid-research. Items marked "Not found in available sources" or "NOT RETRIEVED" are gaps, not findings. File 01 §12 and file 03 §6 list the follow-up searches.
- Prospect ratings (file `prospect-list.csv`) come from public snippets, never from logging into or scraping a platform. No business website could be fetched directly. Every prospect needs the five manual checks in `05-prospect-strategy.md` before contact.
- Name clearance (`raw-name-clearance.md`) is a web sweep, not a legal clearance.

Recommendation: run plumbing, HVAC and roofing as parallel 30-day validation cohorts (10 conversations plus 5 audits each, garage doors as a five-call side bet); the first vertical to sign two paid pilots at A$299 or more wins.

Fifteen deep audits ranked by likelihood of paying, led by Alpha & Omega Air Conditioning (Kirrawee), Pipe Perfection Plumbers (Marrickville) and Ready Electrical & Air Conditioning (Oxford Falls). Two weak rows were replaced; several figures in the first pass were corrected (Ready Electrical is a 4.9/~520 business, not unrated).

## The one-paragraph verdict

Reputrail is a credible, bootstrappable services business, not a venture-scale product. The addressable pool is real and documented: roughly 100,000 trade and home-service businesses in NSW, of which the employing, ≥A$200k-turnover slice that can pay A$299–799 a month is about 25,000 (VERIFIED counts, ESTIMATE on the buying band). Nobody we could find sells a human-managed, owner-approved, trade-specialised review and customer-recovery service in Sydney at this price point; the alternatives are self-serve software the owner never keeps up with, 12-month contracts from US platforms, or SEO agencies that treat reviews as a bolt-on. The moat is thin (relationships, process, local presence) but the economics are good: token costs are under A$3 per customer per month, fixed costs are under A$300 a month, and the binding constraint is the founder's 5–8 hours a week, which caps a solo operation at roughly 8–10 Managed-tier customers (about A$4,000–5,000 MRR) before the first hire or automation step. The business is worth starting if, and only if, the founder enjoys the daily operational grind of reading other people's reviews and making calm phone calls.

## Answers to the fourteen founder questions

1. **Is Reputrail a genuinely attractive business?** Attractive as a profitable owner-operated service (A$50–100k/year contribution at 10–15 customers), with a plausible path to a small agency (A$250–400k revenue at 40–60 customers with one or two staff). Not attractive as a software company: the differentiation is labour and judgement, not code. See `06-commercial-model.md`.
2. **Which trade first?** Plumbing and HVAC, treated as a tie, with roofing third (file 11). File 02 had HVAC narrowly ahead; the briefed weights in file 11 put plumbing narrowly ahead. Run both as validation cohorts and let two paid pilots decide. HVAC ranked first under every weighting in file 02 (highest ability to pay: 64% turn over ≥A$200k; lowest exit rate; recurring servicing; emotional big-ticket reviews). Painters are the best founder fit but 66% are non-employing and only 38% clear A$200k, so A$299+ is a hard sell; use them as design partners and a later lighter tier. Avoid renovators/builders initially: disputes about defects and contracts exceed what a part-time founder should touch. See `02-trade-segmentation.md`.
3. **What is our differentiation?** A named person who reads every review, drafts in the owner's voice, escalates the same day, briefs the owner before the recovery call, and reports monthly; month-to-month terms; an explicit compliance posture (ask everyone, no incentives, no suppression, owner approval on everything public) that the ACCC's own guidance rewards. Nobody at the SMB price point does offline customer recovery. See `03-competitor-analysis.md` Q1–Q3.
4. **What are competitors doing better?** Scale and software: Birdeye and Podium integrate review requests with job-management tools and payments; Google is testing AI-drafted replies inside Business Profile; ServiceM8 and Jobber bundle review requests for A$29–79. They have brand, integrations and 24/7 tooling. Their weaknesses are contracts, cancellation friction and zero human judgement. See `03-competitor-analysis.md` §3–§4.
5. **What should we charge?** Keep A$299/499/799 as the published ladder. It sits level with Birdeye's entry tier, below Podium, and at the floor of agency retainers. Expect Managed (A$499) to be the modal plan for 5–19-staff HVAC and plumbing firms. Price the pilot at A$299 for 30 days of the Managed service, limited to three Sydney businesses, converting to A$499 unless they choose otherwise. Do not go below A$299 for a managed service; a lighter A$149–249 "monitored" tier can be tested with painters later. See `06-commercial-model.md`.
6. **What is the true cost of delivery?** Tokens: roughly US$0.35–1.50 per customer per month at 20–100 reviews (A$0.50–2.30) using Claude Haiku 5.5 for classification and Sonnet 5.5 for drafting; effectively nil. Fixed tooling: about A$60–110 a month (Google Workspace, Vercel Pro, domain, SMS). Insurance and registration: about A$80–130 a month amortised. Labour is the real cost: 2.5–6 founder hours per customer per month depending on tier. Gross margin on cash costs exceeds 90%; on founder time valued at A$100/hour it is 20–55%. See `07-api-cost-model.md` and `06-commercial-model.md`.
7. **How many customers can one person manage?** With 5–8 hours a week and no help: 6–8 Managed customers comfortably, 10 with standing approvals on positive reviews and tight templates. Beyond that, either a part-time virtual assistant (A$25–35/hour) for monitoring, or 10–12 hours a week of founder time. At 15–20 customers the founder is running a small agency and the full-time job is at risk. See `09-service-operating-model.md`.
8. **Can this operate alongside full-time employment?** Yes up to about 10 customers, with two conditions: a same-day (not same-hour) escalation promise, and a fixed daily 20-minute monitoring window before or after work. Nothing in the service requires business-hours availability except customer recovery calls, which the owner makes. Keep the business entirely separate from the employer: own devices, own accounts, no overlap in working hours, no shared contacts.
9. **Biggest risks?** (a) Google ships good-enough AI replies natively and owners stop valuing drafting; (b) the founder's time, not demand, caps growth; (c) a client sues or a customer complains about a reply published under their name (mitigated by approval and insurance); (d) Spam Act exposure from review-request SMS (mitigated by consent, identification, unsubscribe); (e) churn after a quiet winter from seasonal trades; (f) the name has sound-alikes (RepuTrack, RepTrak) that a trade-mark attorney must assess. See `08-compliance-and-risk.md` §8.
10. **Who are the best initial prospects?** The ranked top 15 with individual audits is in `research/prospect-audits/README.md` (v2 supersedes the first-pass top 10): Alpha & Omega Air Conditioning (Kirrawee), Pipe Perfection Plumbers (Marrickville), Ready Electrical & Air Conditioning (Oxford Falls), Priority Plus Plumbing (Kirrawee), Peninsula Air Conditioning (Warriewood), McElroy Plumbing (Randwick), Da-Mell Air Conditioning (Emu Heights), Saunders Electrical Group (Kenthurst), Hardie & Co Plumbing (Warriewood), Shire Air Conditioning (Gymea), Ample Air (Emu Plains), Sanders Roofing (Illawong), Premier Painting Company (Matraville), D'Amore Home Renovations (Northern Beaches), DJ Daisley & Sons (Ashfield). Sutherland Shire Electrical was dropped (its testimonials appear verbatim on a second domain, suggesting a lead-generation network). Every one needs the three Google Maps checks in its audit before contact.
11. **Next 24 hours?** (1) Run the ASIC Connect, ABN Lookup, IP Australia TM Checker and auDA WHOIS checks on "Reputrail" yourself; (2) open Google Maps for the top 10 prospects and read their last 10 reviews and owner replies; (3) text or call three people in your network who know a Sydney plumber or air-conditioning business and ask for one warm introduction each, using the referral script in `business/customer-interview-script.md`; (4) deploy the site to Vercel (preview) and set `AUDIT_WEBHOOK_URL`; (5) book two discovery conversations for this week. See `10-launch-plan.md`.
12. **What should we build next?** Nothing with code until three pilots are live. Then, in order: a Google Sheets-based review log with a Claude-assisted classification script; a Google Business Profile API application once a client base justifies it; a simple client-facing monthly report template. The website only needs a real webhook destination and the founder's name and photo.
13. **What should we absolutely not build?** A dashboard, client logins, payments, automated review scraping, AI auto-replies without approval, any incentive or gating mechanic, a mobile app, or anything that makes Reputrail look like software the client operates. All of it either breaks the positioning, breaks a policy, or costs time that should go to sales.
14. **What evidence would make you abandon the business?** Any of: fewer than 3 of 20 discovery conversations with HVAC/plumbing owners agreeing to a free audit; fewer than 1 of 10 audits converting to a paid pilot; two pilots churning within 60 days citing "I can do this myself"; Google releasing native reply drafting plus review-request automation that owners actually use; or the founder finding after 90 days that the daily monitoring window is being skipped. Set these as explicit kill criteria and review them at day 90.
